I Asked AI to Help Me Build an International Website

A month ago, I had a thought.

It was not a full business plan, nor a finished brand identity. It felt more like a vague sensation: moss, clouds, nature, and a little bit of future technology. I wanted to see what might happen if these things came together.

So I did what many people are doing now: I opened an AI chat and said, “Help me build an international website.”

Looking back, that sentence was rather bold. At the time, I didn’t even know exactly what the site should become.

Many designs are not planned from the beginning. They slowly take shape through repeated attempts until one moment finally feels right.

In the beginning, I could only describe a feeling. I did not provide AI with a complete design document. Most of what I said was uncertain: quieter, not too cold, some Eastern aesthetics, but not like a traditional garden website; some sense of the future, but not like a typical sci-fi movie.

If I had spoken to a designer, they might have asked, “So what exactly do you want?” At that point, I might not have had a clear answer. What was interesting was that AI never seemed bothered by a vague idea. It kept offering new directions.

What about this one? What if we tried another approach? Less technology, more nature? Through those repeated changes, I began to understand my own idea better. I realized I was not simply trying to make a “beautiful website.”

A website slowly became a world.

At first, it was only a few pages. Later, more elements started to appear: a logo, a slogan, a color system, a photographic direction, and even a symbol.

For example, the “~”. At first, it was just a simple curve. Later, I realized it could represent clouds, water vapor, or the soft living rhythm of moss growth. Many design decisions are not discovered at the beginning. They emerge through countless experiments until one moment feels inevitable.

“Yes. That’s it.”

AI did not make the process easy. Many people imagine AI creation like this: type a sentence, click generate, and the result appears. In reality, it is quite different. More often, it sounds like: “No, too commercial.” “Too much like a normal technology website.” “Less future, more life.” “This is good, but it’s not what I’m looking for.” Then you try again.

Sometimes, even a small detail requires many revisions: whether a logo remains legible when reduced, whether the image has enough breathing space, or whether one sentence on the page feels natural. AI can generate many possibilities, but the final decision still comes from human judgment.

Creation is not a single output. It is a process of conversation that gradually gives form to a vague feeling.

I used to think that building an international website required designers, programmers, photographers, writers, and a full team. Now I see that a single person can begin. Of course, that does not mean one person replaces every professional role. It simply means AI has lowered the barrier to starting.

You do not have to wait until everything is ready. One idea, one sketch, or one sentence can become a starting point. Then, through ongoing conversation, it slowly becomes clearer.

Looking back, this website is still far from finished. But it records something interesting: a feeling about clouds and moss gradually grew into its own shape through repeated exchanges between a human and AI.

Perhaps a future brand will not always begin with a business plan. Sometimes, it can begin with a simple sentence: “I have a strange idea.”
